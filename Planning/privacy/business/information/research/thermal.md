Mylar reflects 95% of IR radiation, BUT it has almost zero insulation value (R-value ~0.01).
The Problem:
Heat transfers in 3 ways:
Radiation (IR waves) ← Mylar blocks this 95% ✅
Conduction (through solid materials) ← Mylar does NOT block this ❌
Convection (air currents) ← Not relevant in sealed box



Thermal Signature Timeline (What You're Protecting Against):
Fingertip heat residue on keyboard/touchscreen:
Immediate (0-30 sec): 33-37°C (~95°F) - HIGHLY visible on thermal camera
30-60 seconds: 28-30°C - Still clearly visible
1-2 minutes: 24-27°C - Visible with good thermal camera
2-5 minutes: 22-24°C - Fading, but detectable
5-10 minutes: Nearly ambient (~20-22°C) - Hard to detect
What thermal imaging can reveal:
Which keys were pressed (heat pattern)
Order of keypresses (temperature gradient)
Password length and patterns


Thermal Blocking Requirements:
To prevent external detection of internal 10-15°C temperature differences:
Minimum (Basic Protection):
R-value needed: R-3 to R-5
Temperature masking: Reduces exterior signature to <1°C difference
Detection prevention: Defeats consumer thermal cameras ($300-3k range)
Moderate (Good Protection):
R-value needed: R-8 to R-12
Temperature masking: <0.5°C exterior difference
Detection prevention: Defeats prosumer thermal cameras ($3k-10k)
Maximum (Paranoid/Military):
R-value needed: R-15+
Temperature masking: <0.1°C exterior difference
Detection prevention: Defeats high-end FLIR systems ($10k+)
