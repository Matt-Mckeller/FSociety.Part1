🔆 Sensory Memory

  • Function: Holds raw sensory data
  • Iconic: Visual information
  • Echoic: Auditory information
Duration: < 1 second
Capacity: High

💡 Short-Term (Working) Memory

  • Function: Holds & processes active information
  • Also called: Working Memory
  • Crucial for: Reasoning, comprehension, learning
Duration: < 1 minute
Capacity: ~4 chunks

📚 Long-Term Memory

  • Function: Stores information for later retrieval
  • Two main types: Explicit & Implicit
  • Brain structure: Hippocampus critical for formation
Duration: Years to lifetime
Capacity: Virtually unlimited

🎯 Real-World Examples

👁️Iconic Memory

Example: The brief afterimage you see when you close your eyes after looking at a bright light.

Why it matters: Lets you see continuous motion instead of discrete frames.

👂Echoic Memory

Example: When someone asks "Did you hear what I said?" and you suddenly remember it even though you weren't paying attention.

Why it matters: Brief audio buffer for processing speech.

🔢Working Memory

Example: Remembering a phone number just long enough to dial it, then forgetting it immediately.

Why it matters: Active workspace for mental tasks.

🎂Episodic Memory

Example: Remembering your first day of college, including where you were, how you felt, and what happened.

Type: Explicit/Declarative (conscious recall)

🌍Semantic Memory

Example: Knowing that Paris is the capital of France, or that water boils at 100°C.

Type: Explicit/Declarative (conscious recall)

🚴Procedural Memory

Example: Riding a bike, typing on a keyboard, or tying your shoelaces without consciously thinking about each step.

Type: Implicit/Non-Declarative (unconscious)

🔬 The Famous Case of Patient H.M.

Patient H.M. had his hippocampus surgically removed to treat severe epilepsy. Afterward, he could no longer form new explicit (conscious) long-term memories. He would forget conversations minutes after they happened and couldn't remember people he met post-surgery. However, his implicit memory remained intact—he could still learn new motor skills, even though he had no conscious memory of practicing them! This case proved that explicit and implicit memory systems operate independently and rely on different brain structures.
