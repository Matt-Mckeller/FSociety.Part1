💡 Key Takeaways

1. Constant factors don't matter: O(2n) simplifies to O(n). We care about the shape of growth, not the exact numbers.

2. Drop lower-order terms: O(n² + n) becomes O(n²) because n² dominates as n gets large.

3. Logarithms are your friend: Any algorithm that repeatedly divides the problem in half is O(log n) and extremely fast.

4. Nested loops are a red flag: They usually mean O(n²) or worse. Always look for ways to avoid them with better algorithms or data structures.

5. Real-world impact: For n = 1,000,000, the difference between O(n) and O(n²) is measured in seconds vs. days. Algorithm choice matters!
