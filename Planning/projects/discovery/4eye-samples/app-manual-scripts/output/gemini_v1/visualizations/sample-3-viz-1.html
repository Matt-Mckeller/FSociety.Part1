🎯 Understanding the Memory Process

The journey of a memory involves several stages:

  • Sensory Input: Information enters through our senses (sight, sound, touch, etc.)
  • Sensory Memory: Brief storage (<1 second) of raw sensory data. Most information is lost here unless we pay attention to it.
  • Attention: The critical filter! Only information we attend to moves to short-term memory.
  • Short-Term/Working Memory: Active processing area with limited capacity (~4 chunks) and duration (20-30 seconds). This is where we think and manipulate information.
  • Encoding: The process of transforming information for long-term storage. Requires rehearsal, elaboration, or making connections.
  • Long-Term Memory: Permanent storage with virtually unlimited capacity. Can hold information for years or a lifetime.
  • Retrieval: Bringing information back from long-term memory to working memory when needed.

💡 Key Insight: Memory is not like a video recording. It's an active, constructive process. Each stage involves transformation and potential loss of information. Understanding this flow helps us develop better learning strategies!
