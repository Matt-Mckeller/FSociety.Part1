🧩 Understanding the Memory Process

Information doesn't simply "appear" in our long-term memory. It must travel through a series of processing stages, and at each stage, there's a risk of information being lost.

1️⃣ Sensory Input → Sensory Memory

Everything you see, hear, smell, taste, and touch briefly enters sensory memory. This ultra-brief storage lasts less than a second. Most of it fades immediately unless you pay attention to it.

2️⃣ Attention → Short-Term Memory

Attention is the gateway! When you focus on sensory input, it moves into short-term (working) memory. Here you can actively think about it, manipulate it, and use it. But this storage is limited (~4 chunks) and temporary (< 1 minute).

3️⃣ Encoding → Long-Term Memory

To save information permanently, your brain must encode it into long-term memory. This happens through:

  • Rehearsal: Repeating information (like studying flashcards)
  • Elaboration: Connecting new info to existing knowledge
  • Emotional significance: Strong emotions make memories stick

4️⃣ Retrieval → Back to Working Memory

When you need to use a stored memory, your brain retrieves it from long-term storage back into working memory where you can consciously access and work with it. The hippocampus acts like a "librarian" helping locate and retrieve memories.

❌ Forgetting Happens at Every Stage

Sensory memory: Most information never reaches consciousness—it simply fades.
Short-term memory: Without rehearsal or encoding, information is lost within seconds.
Long-term memory: Even encoded memories can fade or become inaccessible over time (though they may not be permanently deleted).

💡 Study Tip: Use This Knowledge!

To remember information for an exam, you must move it through all stages successfully: Pay attention in class (sensory → short-term), actively work with the material (keep it in working memory), and encode it deeply through practice and connection-making (short-term → long-term). Regular retrieval practice (like self-quizzing) strengthens the pathway and makes future retrieval easier!
