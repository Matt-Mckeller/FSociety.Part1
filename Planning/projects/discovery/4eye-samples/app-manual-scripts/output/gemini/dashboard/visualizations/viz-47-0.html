SN2 Reaction: Concerted Back-side Attack

ANIMATED_SVG very_high effectiveness
Visualization Description
Animation is the most effective way to demonstrate the simultaneous (concerted) nature of the bond-forming and bond-breaking, and the critical 'umbrella flip' inversion of configuration, which is difficult to grasp from static images.
Generation Prompt
Create an animated SVG for a university-level organic chemistry class, illustrating an SN2 reaction. Use a simple substrate like bromomethane (CH3Br). Show a hydroxide ion (OH-) as the nucleophile. The animation should proceed as follows: 1) The OH- ion approaches the carbon atom from the side opposite the Bromine atom (Br). 2) As the OH- gets closer, a dotted line should form to show the new C-OH bond forming, while the C-Br bond, represented by a solid line, begins to stretch and become a dotted line. 3) At the peak of the transition state, the three Hydrogen atoms should be planar with the Carbon. 4) As the Br- ion (leaving group) fully detaches and floats away, the C-OH bond becomes solid, and the three Hydrogen atoms 'flip' to the other side, showing a clear inversion of stereochemistry. Label all species: 'Nucleophile (OH-)', 'Substrate (CH3Br)', 'Transition State', 'Product (CH3OH)', and 'Leaving Group (Br-)'.
