Visualization Description
This complex, multi-stage process is best understood visually. A flowchart can clearly delineate the three stages, their locations within the cell, and the flow of key molecules and energy carriers, which is difficult to track through text alone.
Generation Prompt
Create an SVG diagram for 10th-grade biology illustrating the three stages of cellular respiration. The diagram should have three main sections. Section 1, labeled 'Cytoplasm', shows a Glucose (6C) molecule entering 'Glycolysis', which outputs 2 Pyruvate (3C) molecules, 2 ATP, and 2 NADH. Arrows should show the 2 Pyruvate moving into Section 2, a large mitochondrion shape. Inside the mitochondrion, show a box for the 'Transition Reaction' converting Pyruvate to Acetyl-CoA (2C), releasing CO₂ and creating 2 NADH. This box should lead to Section 3, a circle labeled 'Krebs Cycle' within the 'Mitochondrial Matrix'. The Krebs Cycle should show inputs of Acetyl-CoA and outputs of 4 CO₂, 2 ATP, 6 NADH, and 2 FADH₂. Arrows from all NADH and FADH₂ molecules should point to Section 4, the 'Inner Mitochondrial Membrane', labeled 'Electron Transport Chain'. This section should depict protein complexes, an input of O₂, and outputs of H₂O and a large starburst labeled '~28 ATP'. Use color-coding: blue for molecules, yellow for ATP, orange for NADH/FADH₂. Annotate each stage with its location and net energy products.
